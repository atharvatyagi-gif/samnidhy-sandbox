# Stress-Test Black Box

A local, free simulator for the Samnidhy Sandbox terminal. It runs every strategy the terminal actually contains against a ladder of scenarios,
from a systemic crash to a dead-flat market. It reports the statistics in one Streamlit dashboard and lists every change the results justify.

**SIMULATED RESULTS, NOT PREDICTIONS OF REAL PERFORMANCE.** The markets are synthetic, the scenarios are stylised, and the model-driven strategies
run on a stand-in score (see "Assumptions").

## Run it

```
pip install -r blackbox/requirements.txt
python blackbox/run.py --quick        # 20 seeds a cell, 6 a sweep value: about 2 minutes on 8 cores
python blackbox/run.py                # 200 seeds a cell, 30 a sweep value: the full run (about 15 minutes)
python -m pytest -q blackbox/tests    # 19 tests
```

`run.py` writes the inventory, runs the main grid, the parameter sweeps and the module checks, builds the recommendations, and exports
`results/<run>/report.html` with CSVs of every table. It then opens the dashboard; you can also open it on its own with
`streamlit run blackbox/dashboard/app.py`, using the dark theme from `.streamlit/config.toml`.

- Options: `--no-dashboard`, `--force` (ignore the cache), `--config other.yaml`.
- Results are cached per config hash in `blackbox/results/`, which git ignores.
- A run is reproducible from (config hash + seed).

### The synthetic market

Each stock's daily return is built from:
- a market factor with calm and turbulent volatility regimes
- a sector factor
- Student-t noise (fat tails)
- random jumps
- the scenario's shocks

Volume rises with volatility, and three stock pairs are cointegrated.

## The severity ladder (1 = catastrophic, 10 = flat)

| # | Scenario | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Systemic crash | Index falls 35-50% over 25-50 days. Correlations rise towards 1, spreads widen 5x, 30% of orders fail to fill, and there are three gap-downs. |
| 2 | Flash crash | Intraday low -8 to -12%, close -2 to -4%, partial rebound. Spreads widen 8x and half of orders fail to fill for two days. |
| 3 | War / escalation | Oil +40%, gold +8%, rupee -5%, market -10 to -15%. Energy rises; airlines and consumer fall. Volatility doubles. Heavy news. |
| 3b | Black swan, one stock | The stock the stand-in model likes most gaps down 60% overnight. |
| 4 | Sanctions / trade war | Metals -20%, exporters -12%, auto -10%, oil +15%, market -5 to -8%. |
| 5 | Rate shock | Bond -8%, market -8 to -12%, IT -15%, financials +4%. Bond and equities fall together. |
| 5b | Mixed regimes | Random 30-60-day blocks of bull, chop, bear, mild crash and flat markets. |
| 6 | Banking stress | Financials -25%, market -8 to -12%, volatility x2.5. Spreads widen and some orders fail to fill. |
| 7 | Prolonged bear | -20 to -30% over the year, volatility x1.4. |
| 8 | Sideways chop | A mean-reverting index (half-life 15 days). |
| 9 | Steady bull | +18 to +25% over the year, volatility x0.7. |
| 10 | Dead flat | Drift about 0, volatility x0.3, thin volume, wider spreads. Costs dominate. |

Every scenario is defined in `config.yaml`, so you can edit it. The tests check that the crash really falls 35-50% and that dead flat is really flat.

## Strategies simulated

| Strategy | How it is driven | Mirrors |
|---|---|---|
| `aladin_v3` (the live paper bot), `aladin_v4_shadow`, `aladin_small_10k` | Stand-in score; the trading rules are exact | `scripts/aladin2/paper.py`, `signals.py` |
| `swing_r1` (failed its history gate) | Stand-in score; the price triggers are exact | `scripts/aladin2/swing.py` |
| `movers_top_tenth` (the movers ranking's forward portfolio) | Stand-in score | `scripts/aladin_movers.py` |
| `paper_trader_v1` | Stand-in score | `scripts/paper_trader.py` |
| `crypto_trend` (Bitcoin TREND rule) | Exact, on daily bars | `scripts/crypto_quant.py` |
| `crypto_ha_trend` (Bitcoin Heikin-Ashi rule) | Exact, on daily bars | `scripts/crypto_flow.py` |

Every cell also runs two benchmarks:
- **Buy-and-hold** of the market index (or BTC for the crypto strategies).
- **Random entry:** the same number of trades, the same holding period, the same capital per trade and the strategy's own cost per trade. It isolates timing and selection skill. A strategy that cannot beat it is flagged.

**Module checks:**
- **Advanced-page price signals** (`scripts/live_technicals.py`): their edge over random.
- **ALADIN stand-in calibration.**
- **The terminal's own risk indicators, imported and run as is:** `geo_tension.score_region` and `aladin_sentiment_engine.flow_score`, fed synthetic headlines and flows.
- **Lead/lag:** whether each risk indicator turns before or after the simulated fall (three risk-off days in a row are required), and its false-alarm rate on calm days.

## Assumptions made for every GAP

- **Model-driven strategies use a stand-in score.** The stand-in correlates with each stock's next-k-day return, minus the shocks (no stand-in can foresee a crash).
  - Its strength `ic` is **0.04**, calibrated so that the stand-in's best 1% beats the market by what the real bot's best 1% did (about +0.6% a week over 2,758 trades).
  - The AUC formula would give 0.14. That value is kept in the sweeps as an "AUC-implied" upper case, together with 0 (no skill).
  - The movers stand-in uses **0.06**, which is consistent with its own record.
- **The paper trader's probability** is `0.52 + 0.035 × score`, so about 1% of stocks clear 0.60 on a day.
- **Costs** follow the terminal's table: delivery fees 15.5 bps a side, futures 3 bps, crypto 12 bps, slippage by liquidity decile (2-35 bps). On top of that:
  - half-spread 4 bps
  - slippage scaled by volatility and by the scenario's spread multiplier
  - a liquidity haircut in crises: that share of an order does not fill
  - no borrow fee on stock futures
  - idle cash earns 6.5% minus 0.3% for the ALADIN bots, as in the terminal
- **Gaps:** a gap through a stop fills at the open, never at the stop.
- **Timing:** every decision uses data up to the close of day t; every fill is at t+1. The look-ahead test perturbs everything after t0, checks that nothing before t0 changes, and catches a deliberately leaky strategy.
- **Swept parameters:** every parameter in `config.yaml` `strategies.*.sweep`, one at a time around the terminal's own values. These include position size, number of positions, holding period, stops, profit-taking, the short cap (leverage), cost and slippage multipliers, list thresholds and signal skill. Each value gets a "typical Sharpe" (the median of the per-scenario medians) and a verdict: ROBUST, MIXED, FRAGILE or TERMINAL VALUE OK.

## Design decisions (ambiguities resolved in the simplest, most conservative way)

1. **The terminal is not React.** The brief described React/shadcn/Recharts, but the terminal is vanilla JavaScript plus Python scripts. All trading logic is Python and is re-implemented line by line, citing its source.
2. **No LightGBM.** The allowed dependencies exclude it and the models cannot be retrained on synthetic data, hence the stand-in, calibrated to the real record.
3. **Daily bars for crypto.** The crypto rules run on daily synthetic bars, using the same horizons in days; the terminal uses 1-hour and 4-hour bars.
4. **The paper trader's other conditions are dropped.** The terminal also requires 3/3 front agreement and high confidence, but those fronts do not exist on synthetic data.
5. **News timing is an assumption.** Headlines react on the shock's first day (`news.lead_days` = 0; the war and sanctions scenarios start their news 2 days early). The headline noise level sets the geopolitical index's false-alarm rate. Both are stated wherever they are used.
6. **Recommendations never edit the terminal;** code changes are listed with file paths.

## What could NOT be simulated, and why

- **Fundamental front, screener (Magic Formula / F-score), B-LAB outlook:** they need fundamentals, or have no trading rule.
- **Bitcoin order-flow strategies, crypto ML/CARRY, crypto_adapt:** they need taker-buy volume or funding rates.
- **Sentiment headline scoring (FinBERT):** it needs real text; only the flow score is wired.
- **NEXUS impact, predicted links, supply-chain shocks:** they need a supply-chain graph.
- **Liquidity sweeps:** they need tick data.
- **Globe telemetry layers, news wire:** display only.
- **Futures lot sizes and basis, crypto funding:** not modelled; the terminal does not model them either.

Details and file paths: `INVENTORY.md`.

## Known limitations

- **Synthetic data is not reality.** Real crises have structure (contagion paths, policy responses) that stylised shocks lack.
- **The stand-in scores carry an assumed skill.** The absolute returns of the model-driven strategies depend on it; the `ic` sweep shows the range from no skill to the AUC-implied skill.
- **The cost model is simple.** Market impact does not grow with order size beyond the volume cap.
- **One parameter at a time.** The sweeps miss interactions between parameters.
- **One year per run.** Each evaluated year is a single year after a 260-day warm-up.
