The screen

Each row removes stocks that fail a test. The last bar is today's shortlist. Nothing is hand-picked: the screen re-runs every day, so the list changes as companies' numbers and share prices change.

Rule 1 · Piotroski F-Score (health check)

Nine yes/no questions about the last two annual reports: is the company profitable, is cash coming in, is debt falling, are margins improving, has it avoided issuing new shares, and more. One point per "yes". Only companies scoring 6 or more (out of 9) stay in.

Joseph Piotroski, "Value Investing: The Use of Historical Financial Statement Information", Journal of Accounting Research, 2000.

Rule 2 · Greenblatt's Magic Formula (good + cheap)

Ranks the survivors twice: by return on capital (profit earned on the money the business actually uses) and by earnings yield (profit compared with what the whole company costs). The two ranks are added and the lowest total wins.

Joel Greenblatt, "The Little Book That Beats the Market", 2005. It has beaten the market over long periods in published tests, including on Indian stocks, but not every year.

What the numbers mean

Every signal on this page was chosen because it has been tested on real market data over decades and written up in research. Tested does not mean guaranteed: each one has had long losing stretches.

Trend: 200-day moving average

The average closing price of the last 200 trading days (about ten months). Price above it = long-term uptrend. The 50-day average crossing above the 200-day is called a "golden cross"; below, a "death cross". Evidence: Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992); Faber (2007), who found the rule mainly helped avoid the deepest falls.

Momentum 12-1

The price change from 12 months ago to 1 month ago (the last month is skipped because very recent moves tend to reverse). Stocks with the strongest past-year gains have tended to keep outperforming for several months. Shown as a rank against the NIFTY 500. Evidence: Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), confirmed in many countries including India.

Nearness to the 52-week high

Today's price as a % of the highest price in the last year. Stocks trading close to their 52-week high have tended to do better than those far below it, because investors are slow to believe new highs. Evidence: George & Hwang (2004).

Low volatility

How much the price swings, as a yearly figure. Surprisingly, calmer stocks have historically earned as much as or more than wild ones, with smaller falls: the "low-volatility effect". Evidence: Blitz & van Vliet (2007); Baker, Bradley & Wurgler (2011).

Institutional ownership

The share of the company held by mutual funds, insurers, pension funds and foreign institutions (FIIs). Promoters/insiders are the founders and their group. High institutional ownership means professionals have done their homework, but also that a sale by them can move the price a lot.

Analyst consensus and targets

Brokerage analysts rate stocks from 1 (strong buy) to 5 (sell) and publish 12-month price targets. Useful as a view of expectations, but studies show analysts are on average too optimistic, so treat "upside" as their opinion, not a measurement.

FII / DII flows

Every evening NSE publishes how much foreign institutional investors (FIIs/FPIs) and domestic institutions (DIIs: Indian mutual funds, insurers, banks) bought and sold in the cash market that day. Heavy, persistent FII selling has often come with weak markets in India; DIIs frequently buy when FIIs sell.

India VIX

The market's "fear gauge": how big a move option traders expect in the Nifty over the next month, as a yearly %. Below about 13 is calm, 13 to 18 normal, above 18 nervous, above 25 fearful.

Market breadth

The share of NIFTY 500 stocks trading above their own 200-day average. A rising index with weak breadth means only a few big stocks are carrying it.

Fundamentals vs price signals

The screen picks good, cheap businesses, and they are often cheap because the price has been falling. The price signals describe that trend. When the two disagree, investors weigh value against timing.

Delayed prices

Free prices come from Yahoo Finance a few minutes behind the exchange, and this page updates about every 15 minutes in market hours (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM IST). The time of the last price is always shown.
