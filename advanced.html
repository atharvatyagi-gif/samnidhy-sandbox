The screen

Rule 1 · Piotroski F-Score (health check)

Nine yes/no questions about the last two annual reports: is the company profitable, is cash coming in, is debt falling, are margins improving, has it avoided printing new shares, and more. One point per "yes". Only companies scoring 6 or more (out of 9) stay in.

Joseph Piotroski, "Value Investing: The Use of Historical Financial Statement Information", Journal of Accounting Research, 2000.

Rule 2 · Greenblatt's Magic Formula (good + cheap)

Ranks the survivors twice: by return on capital (profit earned on the money the business actually uses) and by earnings yield (profit compared with what the whole company costs). The two ranks are added, and the lowest total wins.

Joel Greenblatt, "The Little Book That Beats the Market", 2005. Beat the market over long periods in published tests, including on Indian stocks, but not every year.

What the tools mean

No tool here predicts the future. Each one describes something about the past, and traders combine them to judge odds, never certainties.

Heikin-Ashi candles

"Average bar" in Japanese. Each candle blends the day with the one before, so noise is smoothed out. Runs of green candles with no lower wick usually mean a steady uptrend; runs of red with no upper wick, a steady downtrend. Because they are averaged, the candle prices are not the real traded prices.

RSI (Relative Strength Index, 14)

Compares the size of recent up days with recent down days on a 0-100 scale. Above 70 is called "overbought" (rose fast), below 30 "oversold" (fell fast). Strong trends can stay above 70 or below 30 for a long time.

Supertrend (10, 3)

A line that trails the price at 3x the stock's typical daily range (its ATR over 10 days). Price above the line = uptrend (line shown green, under the price); price closing below it flips the trend to down.

Fibonacci retracement

Take the biggest move of the last six months (swing low to swing high, or the other way). Traders watch the 23.6%, 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% "pull-back" points of that move as possible support or resistance. They are reference lines, not laws.

Return on capital

EBIT (profit before interest and tax) divided by the money tied up in the business: working capital plus factories and equipment. Companies that run on customers' money (software, builders taking advances, asset-light brands) can show very high numbers because their capital is small.

Earnings yield

EBIT divided by enterprise value (market value + debt - cash): the profit you'd get per rupee if you bought the whole company, debts included. Higher = cheaper. It's the P/E ratio turned upside down, with debt counted.

Delayed prices

Free prices come from Yahoo Finance a few minutes behind the exchange, and this page is rebuilt about every 15 minutes during market hours (9:15 AM - 3:30 PM IST). The time of the last price is always shown.

Fundamentals vs technicals

The screen picks good, cheap businesses, which are often cheap because the price has been falling. The technicals describe that price action. They often disagree, and that tension is exactly what investors weigh.
